## Story

As a **portfolio researcher**, I want the Exposure tab's ETF look-through
sector breakdown (and the Risk tab's factor exposures, which read the same
sector totals) to show a real sector for every constituent it can actually
identify, and an honest "Unclassified" disclosure — never a guess dressed up
as a classification — for every constituent it cannot, so that I can trust
what I'm looking at instead of discovering later that "Financials" or "Broad
Market" was standing in for "sourced from an ETF ticker that happened to
match a hardcoded keyword."

## Context

Today, when a look-through constituent's own symbol does not resolve through
the static instrument registry, `analytics/risk.py` falls back to one of
three mechanisms, all of which produce a plausible-looking sector label with
no identity evidence behind it, defaulting to the literal `"Other"` when even
the guess fails:

- `_infer_sector_from_sources` (`risk.py:1613-1627`) — a hardcoded
  ETF-ticker-keyword → sector match, used by `build_lookthrough_sector_exposure`.
- `_infer_sector_from_resolved_pair` (`risk.py:1665-1677`) — the same
  hardcoded-keyword shape, used by `_build_shared_sector_overlap` (the
  ETF-overlap-pair card).
- An **ungated** live `market_data.get_company_profile(symbol)` call inside
  `_build_shared_sector_overlap` (`risk.py:1654-1655`) — no identity check of
  any kind, reading FMP's own `sector` field directly.

This is the same fabrication-by-omission problem US-37.1 (Epic 37) fixed for
direct equity holdings, still live in the look-through path. Unlike a
directly-held equity, a look-through constituent — a company discovered by
unpacking an ETF's holdings via `MarketDataService.get_etf_holdings(...)` —
is identified only by a bare ticker string from FMP's own ETF-holdings feed.
It carries no broker-statement ISIN, so US-37.1's identity gate
(statement ISIN vs. FMP profile ISIN) has no applicable input here: per the
research brief, this is not an edge case of that gate, it is the *only*
reachable outcome for every look-through constituent, on every run
(`03-quant-research.md` § Why constituents cannot reuse the ISIN gate
literally). A same-provider round trip — asking FMP "what sector is this
ticker" using an identity FMP itself already supplied — is
`provider-self-consistent`, not independent evidence, and this story does not
invent a weaker tier that pretends otherwise.

**The required rule (quant research brief, binding on this story's ACs).**
Per-source-slice attribution is unchanged: each ETF (or direct position)
contributing value to a constituent classifies independently, so one ETF's
look-through value can legitimately split across a real sector and
"Unclassified" in the same response. A slice with a resolvable sector
(via the static registry, or — unaffected by this story — the pre-existing
curated fund-category override for Thematic/Sector/Bond/Commodity ETFs)
counts toward that sector. A slice that cannot be resolved counts, at its
full weight, toward "Unclassified" — never redistributed pro-rata across the
resolved sectors, and never dropped from the total or the weight denominator
(`03-quant-research.md` § Proposed aggregation rule, § Worked example).

**What is explicitly unaffected.** The per-source **fund-category override**
— a curated, static registry field, e.g. a Bond/Commodity/Sector/Thematic ETF
with its own `.sector` set (`SLV`, `ICOM`, `SGLD`, `ISLN`, `IEF` are already
curated this way) — is a different mechanism from the three fallback sites
above and is not touched by this story. It is the same trust tier as
US-37.1's `"static"` tier: human-reviewed data, no identity gate needed.
Conflating it with the two hardcoded-list functions being removed would
regress correctly-resolved fund-category labels that have nothing to do with
the fabrication problem this story addresses (`03-quant-research.md` § What is
NOT in scope).

**Suppression-threshold exemption (resolved by the human).** The existing
`MIN_SECTOR_WEIGHT` (0.05%) display-suppression filter in
`build_lookthrough_sector_exposure` today omits any bucket below that weight
from the itemized response list entirely (the weight still counts in the
denominator; it is just not itemized). The human resolved this in favor of
**exempting "Unclassified" from that filter**: however small, "Unclassified"
is always itemized in the response, matching the direct-equity "Unclassified"
precedent this story extends (`overview.py`, US-37.1), which has no
equivalent floor. Every other, resolved sector bucket keeps the existing
`MIN_SECTOR_WEIGHT` filter unchanged — this exemption is specific to
"Unclassified" (`03-quant-research.md` § Open decision: suppression
threshold; human resolution carried into AC8 below).

**Companion registry curation (resolved by the human — ships inside this
story).** The research brief verified eight ETF tickers — `XLF`, `XLV`,
`IBB`, `ITA`, `PPA`, `BIL`, `VGSH`, `DBC` — are referenced by the fallback
logic this story removes but are **not** curated in the static registry
today, so removing the fallback with no companion action would move any
look-through value sourced *only* through one of these eight funds from a
guessed sector label to "Unclassified" — correct per this story's
aggregation rule, but a real, potentially sizeable shift if any of these
funds are material in the bound statement. The human resolved this in favor
of shipping the companion curation of all eight tickers into
`INSTRUMENT_DEFINITIONS` inside this story, not as a fast-follow
(`03-quant-research.md` § Companion recommendation; human resolution carried
into AC9 and T-38.1.2 below).

Implementer must also read: `docs/product/stories/US-37.1-dynamic-equity-sector-classification.md`
(the sibling story this extends the pattern from — same identity-gate
principle, same "Unclassified" bucket precedent); `03-quant-research.md` in
full, especially § Proposed aggregation rule, § Worked example, § Explicit

contract rows, including the direct-equity "Unclassified" precedent this story
extends into the look-through path).

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] AC1 — For a look-through constituent whose own symbol resolves through
  the static instrument registry, its sector classification is unchanged by
  this story.
- [x] AC2 — For a look-through constituent sourced (in full or in part)
  through a curated Thematic/Sector/Bond/Commodity ETF with its own `.sector`
  set in the static registry, that slice's classification by the fund's own
  curated sector is unchanged by this story.
- [x] AC3 — For a look-through constituent whose symbol does not resolve
  through the static registry, and whose sourcing ETF is not a curated
  fund-category override, the affected slice of that constituent's value is
  disclosed under a distinct "Unclassified" sector label — never the literal
  string `"Other"`, and never a sector name that looks like a real
  classification but is actually a guess derived from the sourcing ETF's
  ticker (`analytics/risk.py`, `build_lookthrough_sector_exposure`).
- [x] AC4 — The same "Unclassified" treatment in AC3 applies to the
  ETF-overlap-pair card's shared-symbol sector attribution
  (`_build_shared_sector_overlap`), including the case that today falls
  through to the ungated live FMP profile lookup rather than a hardcoded
  keyword guess.
- [x] AC5 — No live `get_company_profile` (or any other market-data) call is
  made to classify a look-through constituent's or shared-symbol's sector when
  the static registry does not resolve it — the "Unclassified" outcome is
  reached without a network/cache round trip, since no identity evidence
  exists for such a call to be gated on in the first place.
- [x] AC6 — When only some of an ETF's look-through constituents (or slices
  of one constituent's value) resolve a real sector, the ETF's total
  look-through value still reconciles exactly across the returned sector
  buckets (including "Unclassified") — no value is dropped from the total
  market value or the weight denominator, and no unresolved slice's weight is
  redistributed across the sectors that did resolve.
- [x] AC7 — The Risk tab's factor-exposure sector tilts, which read the same
  underlying sector totals `build_lookthrough_sector_exposure` produces, do
  not silently zero out a real, non-fabricated exposure as an unlabeled side
  effect of this story — any factor-tilt figure that changes because value
  that was previously mislabeled now reads "Unclassified" is a legitimate,
  expected consequence of this fix, not a regression to hide or work around.
- [x] AC8 — The "Unclassified" bucket in the look-through sector response is
  always itemized, however small its weight — it is exempt from the existing
  `MIN_SECTOR_WEIGHT` (0.05%) display-suppression filter that every other
  sector bucket remains subject to, matching the direct-equity "Unclassified"
  precedent (`overview.py`, US-37.1), which has no equivalent floor.
- [x] AC9 — For a look-through constituent sourced (in full or in part)
  through one of the eight companion-curated ETF tickers (`XLF`, `XLV`,
  `IBB`, `ITA`, `PPA`, `BIL`, `VGSH`, `DBC`), that slice classifies by the
  ETF's curated sector/category in `INSTRUMENT_DEFINITIONS` rather than
  landing in "Unclassified".

## Test plan

Backend (pytest), likely `test_risk.py` or a dedicated module for the
look-through sector resolution (final location is the tech lead's /
backend-engineer's call):

- Static-registry fast path unaffected — a look-through constituent whose
  symbol is in `INSTRUMENT_DEFINITIONS` resolves exactly as it does today,
  with no market-data call made for its classification.
- Curated fund-category override unaffected — a slice sourced through a
  curated Thematic/Sector/Bond/Commodity ETF still classifies by the fund's
  own `.sector`, not "Unclassified".
- Unresolved-constituent case — a look-through constituent with no registry
  hit, sourced through a non-curated ETF, lands in "Unclassified" with its
  full slice value counted (not dropped, not pro-rated).
- Partial-resolution case (the worked example's shape) — one constituent
  resolving to a real sector and another, in the same response, resolving to
  "Unclassified," with the total market value and weight sum reconciling
  across both.
- No-network-call regression — classifying an unresolved look-through
  constituent's or shared-symbol's sector does not call
  `MarketDataService.get_company_profile` (or any other market-data method).
- `_build_shared_sector_overlap` coverage mirroring the above for the
  ETF-overlap-pair card, including the previously-ungated live-profile
  fallback path.
- Regression — deleting `_infer_sector_from_sources` and
  `_infer_sector_from_resolved_pair` does not change any already-resolved
  (registry-hit or fund-category-override) classification anywhere in the
  bound statement's own fixtures.
- Suppression-exemption regression — a small "Unclassified" bucket (below
  the 0.05% `MIN_SECTOR_WEIGHT` threshold) is itemized in the response
  rather than folded into the denominator-only remainder, while a comparably
  small *resolved* sector bucket in the same fixture remains subject to the
  existing filter.
- Companion-registry-curation coverage — each of the eight newly-curated
  tickers (`XLF`, `XLV`, `IBB`, `ITA`, `PPA`, `BIL`, `VGSH`, `DBC`) resolves
  its curated sector/category for look-through value sourced through it,
  rather than "Unclassified".

Frontend (vitest): none anticipated by default — `ExposurePanel.tsx`'s
look-through sector rendering is data-driven off whatever bucket labels the
engine returns (mirroring US-37.1's own reasoning for not ticketing frontend
work by default). If the design pass decides "Unclassified" needs its own
visual treatment on the look-through card specifically, a frontend ticket
should be added at that point.

Regression / guardrail:

- No-fabrication regression: a look-through constituent or shared symbol with
  no resolvable sector is never assigned a sector string standing in for
  absence (neither `"Other"` nor a plausible-looking-but-guessed real sector
  name).
- `python scripts/run_all_tests.py` green. Goldens diff inspected: this story
  ships the companion registry curation for the eight named tickers (`XLF`,
  `XLV`, `IBB`, `ITA`, `PPA`, `BIL`, `VGSH`, `DBC`) in the same pass, so
  look-through value sourced through them should keep resolving to a real
  sector rather than shifting to "Unclassified". Any *other*, non-curated
  look-through constituent in the bound statement's own fixtures that
  reclassifies from a guessed sector to "Unclassified" is the legitimate
  flow-through of this fix and should be committed, not treated as a
  failure.

## Out of scope

- **Re-deriving ETF-level sector classification from FMP directly.** FMP's
  `sector` field for an ETF ticker itself returns the fund sponsor's own
  classification, not a thematic category (US-37.1's research verified both
  `SPY` and `GRID` return `"Financial Services"`) — this story is about the
  constituent-level fallback, not inventing a new ETF-level source.
- **A new dynamic, identity-gated FMP tier for look-through constituents**,
  analogous to US-37.1's tier 2. Structurally inapplicable: a look-through
  constituent carries no statement ISIN, on any run, so the identity evidence
  such a tier would need to be trustworthy does not exist for this input.
- **The `industry` field**, or any FMP field beyond `sector`. Not required by
  this story.
- **Any change to `LookThroughConstituent`'s or `EtfOverlapPair`'s schema
  shape** beyond what is required to carry the "Unclassified" label — field
  names, types, and nullability are the tech lead's design-pass call, not
  fixed by this story.

  shape (tier-1-only static lookup, tier-2 treated as structurally
  inapplicable) is the tech lead's / backend-engineer's call, not specified
