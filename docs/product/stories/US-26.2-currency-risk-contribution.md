# US-26.2: Currency risk contribution (historical)

**Epic:** Epic 26 — Currency Exposure & Risk
**PRD:** [`epic-26-currency-exposure-and-risk.md`](../prd/epic-26-currency-exposure-and-risk.md)
**Research brief:** [`currency-risk-contribution-brief.md`](../../finance/research/currency-risk-contribution-brief.md)
**Status:** Done
**Last updated:** 2026-08-11

## Story

As a **portfolio researcher**, I want to see how much of my portfolio's return
volatility came from **currency moves** rather than from the securities
themselves, so that I can tell whether my 21% non-USD exposure is actually
driving my risk or is just sitting there quietly.

## Context

US-26.1 shipped *composition* — how much of the portfolio is in each currency.
This ships *consequence* — whether that exposure did anything.

Every return series in the product is already in base currency, so the FX
component is baked in and invisible. The rolling correlation/beta surfaces
measure co-movement with an equity benchmark, not with FX. Nothing decomposes a
return into its security and currency legs.

Both blockers that held this story at "not ready" are resolved by the research
brief and recorded in methodology §Currency Risk Contribution:

- **Data prerequisite — verified.** `get_fx_history` returns real FX series;
  `resolve_symbol_candidates("EURUSD", kind="history")` is `['EURUSD']` with no
  equity fallback, so the US-31.4 wrong-instrument class does not apply.
- **Interaction term — resolved, and the old formula was wrong.** The
  decomposition is an **exact identity**, not an approximation. The cross term
  is negligible per day (≤5.4bp) but **compounds** to **+0.504pp** of cumulative
  return on SEMI over six months, so it is retained and reported as its own
  third leg.
- **Variance decomposition — resolved.** `Var(r_p)` splits exactly by
  bilinearity of covariance into three component shares summing to 1.0.

**Wiring correction to the brief.** The brief suggested attaching this to the
exposure engine. That is wrong: `exposure_engine.py` fetches **no** historical
prices — it is a pure snapshot engine, and this analytic needs windowed price
*and* FX history. Attaching it there would mix snapshot analytics with
synthetic history in one response (guardrail #2) and would slow every Exposure
request even when the card is not viewed. This story uses the established
**dedicated route + self-fetching card** pattern instead
(`FactorAttributionCard`, `BenchmarkCorrelationTable`, and the whole Risk tab).

Implementer must read: `financial-methodology.md` §Currency Risk Contribution
(the formulas are final — do not re-derive); the research brief;
`app/analytics/currency.py` (base-currency weights);
`app/services/attribution_engine.py` (the windowed synthetic-history pattern +
`_lookback_calendar_days`); `app/core/constants.py` (`MIN_DAILY_OBSERVATIONS`);
`app/instruments/registry.py` (fund currency, US-31.5);
`.claude/skills/ui-polish/SKILL.md`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] AC1 — The Exposure tab shows a **Currency Risk Contribution** card giving
  the share of portfolio return variance attributable to the **local** leg, the
  **currency** leg and the **interaction** leg, for a selectable 60d/252d window.
- [x] AC2 — **The three shares sum to exactly 1.0** (within float tolerance),
  because they are component covariances of an exact identity — asserted by
  test, not by comment.
- [x] AC3 — **The per-holding identity holds exactly:**
  `L(t) + F(t) + X(t) == Σ_i w_i × r_base_i(t)` for every day in the window.
  This is the pin that catches a leg being computed from the wrong currency.
- [x] AC4 — **The interaction leg is reported, never folded away.** It appears
  as its own share, even when tiny; it is not allocated to the currency leg
  (the Ankrim–Hensel convention this story deliberately departs from).
- [x] AC5 — **A negative share renders as negative.** A currency leg moving
  against the local leg genuinely reduces portfolio variance; the share is
  never clamped to zero, and the card explains what a negative share means.
- [x] AC6 — **Local returns use the registry fund currency** (US-31.5), not the
  broker's listing currency — a holding listed EUR but quoting USD contributes
  **zero** FX leg. Pinned in both directions.
- [x] AC7 — **Fail-closed on thin data:** fewer than `MIN_DAILY_OBSERVATIONS`
  (20) paired (price, FX) days → every share, volatility and correlation is
  `null` and the card shows its EmptyState. Never a confident number from a
  handful of days.
- [x] AC8 — **A holding with no fund-currency price history is excluded and
  disclosed by symbol**, with the excluded weight reported. It is never assigned
  to the local leg at zero FX, which would silently understate currency risk.
- [x] AC9 — **Zero-variance guards:** `Var(r_p) = 0` → all shares null (never 0
  or 1); either leg with zero variance → `local_fx_correlation` null.
- [x] AC10 — The card carries a **`Synthetic` TrustBadge** — this applies
  current holdings to historical prices, unlike the US-26.1 composition card,
  and the schema's trust field is `'synthetic' | 'unavailable'`, never
  `verified`.
- [x] AC11 — A **base-currency-only portfolio** yields a currency share of
  exactly 0.0 and a local share of exactly 1.0 — correct in the trivial case.
- [x] AC12 — `dashboardGoldens.ts` is byte-identical (new Exposure surface; no
  dashboard-history output changes).

## Notes / decisions

- **The formulas are final and live in the methodology doc.** Do not re-derive
  them from the brief — implement §Currency Risk Contribution verbatim. The
  brief is the *evidence*; the methodology section is the *contract*.
- **Why the interaction leg is separate.** Ankrim–Hensel conventionally
  allocates the cross term to currency. Doing that here would overstate the
  currency leg by an amount the researcher cannot see. Three legs summing
  exactly to the base return is the honest construction, and it matches how
  this project already surfaces residuals it cannot cleanly attribute (the
  drawdown "Residual (unexplained)" row; US-31.3's recorded
  `reconciliation_adjustment`).
- **Why component-covariance, not `Var(F)/Var(r_p)`.** The naive ratio ignores
  `Cov(L, F)` and does not sum to 1, so the shares would not account for the
  portfolio. The covariance form is the same identity `risk.py` already uses
  for factor and position risk-share.
- **The fund-currency trap is the highest implementation risk.** US-31.5 proved
  the broker's listing currency is *not* the quote currency (DEFS listed EUR,
  `DEFS.L` quotes USD) and that using the listing currency made drift **4.3×
  worse**. A wrong assignment here silently moves return between the legs and
  the identity check (AC3) will *still pass*, because both legs come from the
  same wrong split. AC6's two-directional pin is the guard.
- **Academic precedent** (already in the methodology section):
  - Ankrim & Hensel (1994), *JPM* 20(2), 35–41 — local/currency decomposition.
  - Solnik (1974), *JET* 8(4), 500–524 — currency as a distinct priced exposure.
  - Perold & Schulman (1988), *FAJ* 44(3), 45–50 — reporting the currency leg
    separately.
  - Menchero & Davis (2011), *JPM* 37(2), 97–107 — the component-contribution
    identity behind the variance split.


## What implementation changed (recorded at close-out)

**Request shape corrected during build.** The schema first extended the flat
`PortfolioEngineRequest` (the drift/stress shape). That is wrong here:
base-currency weights come from `position_base_market_values`, which needs the
statement's own implied `fx_rates` — and the flat shape only materialises those
when rates are supplied. Switched to the **snapshot-wrapped** request the
sibling Exposure engines already use (attribution, correlation), so the weights
are computed against real broker rates rather than an unconverted sum.

**A project guard caught the new route before the suite did.**
`test_engine_response_integrity.py::test_all_engine_run_routes_are_covered`
requires every `/engines/*/run` route to carry response-integrity coverage or
an explicit waiver with a reason. The new route was added to `ENGINE_ROUTES`
rather than waived.

**Measured on the committed portfolio** (60d window, live FMP data, verified
through the running route):

| Leg | Share of return variance |
|---|---|
| Securities (local) | **96.70%** |
| Currency | **3.31%** |
| Interaction | **−0.013%** |
| **Sum** | **exactly 1.0** |

Per-currency contributions reconcile to the headline: EUR 0.024268 + GBP
0.008854 = 0.033122 against a currency share of 0.033121. The interaction share
came out genuinely **negative** and was rendered unclamped, exercising AC5 on
real data rather than only in a constructed test. Standalone vols: securities
18.76%, currency 2.51%, correlation 0.127.

**The answer for this portfolio:** the 21% non-USD exposure contributes about
**3.3%** of return variance — real but small next to the securities themselves.
