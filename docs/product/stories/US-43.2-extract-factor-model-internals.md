# US-43.2: Extract the factor-model internals out of `risk.py`

**Epic:** Epic 43 — Engine Seam Consolidation
**PRD:** [`epic-43-engine-seam-consolidation.md`](../prd/epic-43-engine-seam-consolidation.md)
**Status:** Done
**Last updated:** 2026-09-02

## Story

As a **developer of this project**, I want the statistical factor model's
internals — fit, factor orthogonalisation, the factor definitions and proxy
maps — to live in `analytics/factor_model.py` instead of being private helpers
inside the 2,300-line `analytics/risk.py` that other modules import across the
seam, so that the factor-model concern is one navigable file and no consumer
reaches past `risk.py`'s interface into its `_private` names.

## Context

`analytics/risk.py` presents ~30 public `build_*` entry points across six
unrelated concerns. Two modules already reach *past* that interface:

- `analytics/attribution.py:28` imports `_fit_factor_model`,
  `_orthogonalize_factors_window`, `_selected_history_return_series` **(private)**
  plus `DEFAULT_FACTOR_DEFINITIONS`, `FACTOR_KEY_MAP`, `FACTOR_PROXY_MAP`,
  `ROLLING_RIDGE_FLOOR`, `ReturnBasis`.
- `services/stress_engine.py:18` imports `STRESS_SCENARIOS` and
  `build_statistical_factor_model`.

This story does the **leak-first** slice only: the factor-model internals that
are imported across the seam (plus their immediate private helpers and the
factor-definition data) move to `analytics/factor_model.py`; `risk.py` imports
them back and keeps its response-shaping entry points
(`build_statistical_factor_model`, `build_factor_shift_diagnostics`, …) where
they are. The remaining five `risk.py` concerns are **not** touched — a full
split is a tracked follow-up.

Behaviour-neutral: the code moves verbatim, so the factor model, attribution,
and stress outputs and the goldens stay byte-identical.

Recorded in `docs/tech-debt-register.md` as row US-43.2.

## Notes / decisions

- **`risk.py` keeps `build_statistical_factor_model`.** The leak-first rule
  moves only what crosses the seam today plus its immediate private helpers and
  the factor-definition data. The public entry point staying in `risk.py` while
  importing from `factor_model.py` is an acceptable transitional shape; the full
  split can finish the move later.
- **`ReturnBasis` home = `schemas/return_basis.py`.** It is shared vocabulary
  already co-located with the `ReturnBasis*` family, and `schemas` is the
  contract source of truth. `analytics` importing from `schemas` is the correct
  layering direction.
- **This is the riskiest story in the epic on the test side.** `test_analytics.py`
  monkeypatches `risk_module` internals by name; the AC5 identity test exists to
  make the retarget provably correct rather than hopeful.
- No new formula; a relocation, not a methodology change.
