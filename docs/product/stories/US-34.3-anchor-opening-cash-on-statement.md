# US-34.3: Anchor opening cash on the statement's own starting cash

**Epic:** Epic 34 — An Answerable Dashboard: Reachable Trust States
**PRD:** [`epic-34-answerable-dashboard-and-reachable-trust.md`](../prd/epic-34-answerable-dashboard-and-reachable-trust.md)
**Status:** Done (2026-08-13)
**Last updated:** 2026-08-13

## Story

As a **portfolio researcher**, I want the replay's opening cash taken from the
figure my broker actually reported, so that the standing "opening cash is
degraded" warning clears when nothing is wrong and means something when it
fires.

## Context

PRD finding **F-2**, and most of **F-5**.

The replay derives opening cash as
`base_cash = starting_nav − opening_positions_value`. The two terms are dated
differently by construction: `starting_nav` is as of the **statement-period
start** (2026-01-01) while the positions are valued at the **replay window
start** (2026-01-08, the first date the replay can value). Five trading days of
market movement are absorbed into cash as a plug, the anchor reports `degraded`
with a **−$1,377.59** residual, and — because those two dates coincide only if
an account happens to trade on the first day of its statement period — that
warning fires on **every run of every statement**. A disclosure that cannot
clear trains the researcher to ignore the ones that matter, which is the
opposite of what `ReplayDisclosuresCard` exists for.

**The statement already reports the answer.** `ImportedCashBalance.starting_cash`
carries the broker's own opening cash per currency — on IB2026, USD $4,672.04
with EUR and GBP at zero. It is broker truth, exactly dated at the period start,
and needs no market data. Measured:

| | Derived (today) | Statement's `starting_cash` |
|---|---|---|
| Opening cash | $3,252.74 | **$4,672.04** |
| Residual vs ledger-implied | −$1,377.59 | **+$46.69** (97% smaller) |
| Terminal reconciliation | +$1,366.17 | **−$58.11** (96% smaller) |

**Owner decisions (2026-08-13).** (1) Use the statement's reported starting cash,
falling back to the derived identity when a statement does not report one — not
the alternative of extending the valuation window back to the period start,
which needs a `golden_market_data.json` re-capture and still relies on a derived
identity. (2) The anchor's trust reflects its **source**: directly observed
broker truth is `verified`, with the residual reported alongside as a
reconciliation check rather than as a reason to degrade.

Implementer must read:
- PRD F-2 and F-5.
- `app/engine/portfolio_state.py` → `_classify_cash_anchor`,
  `_statement_implied_opening_cash`, and the `base_cash` block in
  `build_daily_states`.
- `docs/contracts/dashboard-fields.md` → `run_metadata.replay_cash_anchor`.
- `docs/finance/financial-methodology.md` §Synthetic History Coverage Rule
  (where the replay's anchoring rules are documented).

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] AC1 — **Opening cash comes from the statement when the statement reports
  it.** The replay anchors on the sum of `ImportedCashBalance.starting_cash`
  across currencies, each FX-converted at the window start. On IB2026 day-one
  cash is **$4,672.04**, not the derived $3,252.74.

- [x] AC2 — **The derivation survives as a fallback, unchanged.** A statement
  with no reported starting cash still anchors on
  `starting_nav − opening_positions_value` and still reports the existing
  `statement_nav_at_window_start` / `statement_nav_date_mismatch` bases. The
  request path (no `statement_totals`) still anchors on the snapshot's own cash
  balances. Neither path changes by a cent.

- [x] AC3 — **The anchor can now be `verified`, and on IB2026 it is.** A new
  `statement_starting_cash` basis reports trust `verified`, because the value is
  directly observed broker truth rather than a derived identity. The standing
  warning clears: `ReplayDisclosuresCard` renders **no** cash-anchor note on the
  committed statement.

- [x] AC4 — **The residual is still measured and still published.** The anchor
  carries a **+$46.69** residual against the ledger-implied opening cash
  (`cash_total` less FX-converted flows), reported alongside the `verified`
  trust as a reconciliation check. Trust reflects the source; the residual
  reports how well the ledger reconciles the two cash endpoints — they are
  different facts and both are surfaced.

- [x] AC5 — **A materially unreconciled statement still degrades.** When the
  residual exceeds a documented share of opening cash, the basis stays
  `statement_starting_cash` but the trust drops to `degraded` and the card shows
  the note — so the disclosure remains a real signal, not one that is now
  permanently silent instead of permanently loud.

- [x] AC6 — **The FX-converted-flows trap is not reintroduced.** The residual's
  ledger-implied term keeps using **FX-converted** flows (US-31.3 AC3). A test
  pins that the raw currency-mixed sum is still the wrong basis.

- [x] AC7 — **The terminal reconciliation shrinks by the anchor correction.**
  On IB2026 the adjustment moves **+$1,366.17 → −$58.11**, i.e. 96% of it was
  the anchor offset riding through the window. `total_market_value` on every day
  is **unchanged** — this story moves cash, not valuations.

- [x] AC8 — **F-5 is reduced, not closed, and the story says so.** At −$58.11
  the adjustment is still above `REPLAY_RECONCILIATION_TOLERANCE`, so the
  terminal day's return remains withheld and `withheld_return_dates` still
  contains it. No acceptance criterion claims otherwise.

- [x] AC9 — **Every published figure moves consistently.** Day-one and every
  subsequent `total_portfolio_value` rise by the anchor correction, so
  `start_value`, the money-weighted return and the investment gain all move
  together and remain internally consistent. The time-weighted return changes
  only through the denominators it was already using.

## Out of scope

  derived identity is correctly dated. It needs a `golden_market_data.json`
  re-capture (FMP key) and remains a derived identity even then. Logged
  separately; the owner chose the statement anchor for this story.
- **Closing F-5.** The terminal day stays withheld (AC8). Whether a −$58.11
  adjustment should still cost a day is a tolerance question, and the terminal
  day's publishability is already tracked as F-8 / US-34.8.
- **Changing `REPLAY_RECONCILIATION_TOLERANCE`** or the terminal reconciliation
  itself.
- The opening **positions** side of the anchor — this story changes only how
  opening cash is obtained.

## Notes / decisions

- **No new formula, and no academic citation applies.** This is a data-source
  precedence rule: prefer the broker's reported figure over an identity derived
  from two differently-dated terms. The methodology entry states the precedence
  and why, alongside the existing US-31.3 anchor discussion.
- **Why trust follows the source, not the residual.** The current rule degrades
  on any residual above $1.00, which is right for a *derived* anchor — a
  residual there means the derivation is absorbing something. For a *directly
  observed* anchor the residual measures something different: how well the
  ledger's flows reconcile the statement's own two cash endpoints. That is worth
  publishing, but it is not evidence the opening cash is wrong. AC5 keeps a
  degrade path so the distinction cannot become a blanket "always verified".
- **The AC5 threshold needs a value and a rationale.** Measured margin on
  IB2026: residual $46.69 on $4,672.04 opening cash = **1.0%**. Pick the
  constant with that margin stated, as a heuristic policy value with no academic
  basis (US-24.2 discipline), and put it in `app/core/constants.py` — not
  inline.
- **Expect a large, benign golden diff.** Every daily cash and portfolio value
  moves by the anchor correction. The characterisation in T-34.3.5 is what
  separates that from a regression, so it is not optional.
