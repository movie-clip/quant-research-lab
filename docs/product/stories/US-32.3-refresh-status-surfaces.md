## Story

As a **developer or agent returning to this project**, I want the first
paragraph of the roadmap and the pointers in CLAUDE.md to describe the project
as it is today, so that I can answer "what's open, what shipped last?" without
reading a 900-line slice log to discover the summary was wrong.

## Context

**F-2.** CLAUDE.md names **Epic 13** as "the most-recent shipped epic PRD" in

## Outcome (2026-08-19)

**The story had gone stale before it was built, which is the finding.** AC1 was
written on 2026-08-12 and asked the roadmap to state "every epic Completed,
Epic 26 most recently shipped, and the open items named". Four epics have
shipped since (31, 32, 33, 34), and the roadmap's summary line still read
*Updated: 2026-07-18 … Epic 31 active*. The AC's **intent** — "no epic is
described as active when it is not" — is exactly right and is what was
implemented, against today's truth rather than the AC's 2026-08-12 example.

**Two epics were closed as part of it.** Epic 34 and Epic 32 both still carried
`## Active Epic:` headings, and Epic 34's problem statement was written in the
present tense ("time-weighted return … **`null` in all five ranges, on every
run**") — a description of the bug the epic *fixed*, reading as a live report of
the product. Both headings moved to `## Completed Epic:`, both problem
statements are now marked as describing the state that prompted the epic, and
Epic 34's section states where it ended.

**AC2/AC3 changed the pointer's shape, not just its target.** CLAUDE.md named
Epic 13's PRD as "most recent shipped epic", which went stale the moment Epic 14
shipped — the same failure the story is about. It now sends the reader to
`epic-roadmap.md` **first** and names the current PRD as a convenience, saying
explicitly that a named PRD here goes stale every time an epic ships.

**AC6 was a genuine contradiction, not just wording.** `build-story`'s close-out
said "Commit … Squash-merge; prune local + remote branch" unconditionally, which
tells an agent to publish without being asked. It now defers to the user.

**The open-items list is the part most likely to matter next**, since it is what
a reader lands on: the two tech-debt rows, the Epic 34 findings deliberately
left open (F-1a, F-10's drawdown half, F-12), and the unticketed cache hazard
US-34.9 found — `manage_cache.py clear` does not cover the `history_yf`
namespace, and the FMP client persists 401/402/403/404 as an empty result, so a
transient failure becomes a durable one.
